# Greek Cities in Present-Day West Asia

An interactive map, statistics and the full list of the cities whose site lies in present-day West Asia (Cyprus, Syria, Lebanon, Israel and Palestine, the Golan Heights, Jordan, Iraq and Iran)
and that became Greek, or were bilingual with many Greek speakers beside their own language, between the first Greek settlers in Cyprus (11th century BC) and the Arab conquest (634–651),
with when the last Greek speakers were gone from each; the Patriarchates of Jerusalem and Antioch, which outlived the mainland cities; and Cyprus, where Greek is spoken still.

**Open the page:** https://iosiflazaridis.github.io/greek-cities-west-asia/

Present-day Turkey is not included: its Greek cities, Antioch, Edessa and Tarsus among them, are on a page of their own, [Greek Cities in Present-Day Turkey](https://iosiflazaridis.github.io/greek-cities-turkey/).
Egypt is not included either: the Greek cities of Sinai, the part of Egypt that lies in Asia (Pelusium, Rhinocorura, Pharan and others), are listed with the rest of Egypt in [Greek Cities in Present-Day Africa](https://iosiflazaridis.github.io/greek-cities-africa/).
Georgia, whose Greek cities lay on its Black Sea coast, is also left out.
The Greek cities east of Iran, in Afghanistan, Turkmenistan and the rest of Central and South Asia, are in [Greek Cities of Central and South Asia](https://iosiflazaridis.github.io/greek-cities-central-south-asia/).

- `index.html`: the page (self-contained; the data are embedded)
- `img/`: the eleven images in full size; `thumb/`: their thumbnails
- stories from 26 of the cities (their philosophers, poets and scholars, and events) are part of the page and of the data files
- `dl/`: the data as CSV, JSON and an Excel workbook (with the places left out, with reasons, the year-by-year series, the capitals on the sites, the sees of the two patriarchates and the Greek communities after 1800)

Sources: the Inventory of Archaic and Classical Poleis (Hansen & Nielsen 2004); G. M. Cohen, The Hellenistic Settlements (2006, 2013); the ancient authors, coins and inscriptions;
Hierocles and the lists of bishops; the Pleiades gazetteer; Encyclopaedia Iranica; for the later Greeks, the two patriarchates, Griffith (2008), Soviet and Armenian censuses and the Greek Foreign Ministry.
Every record in the data keeps its own sources and notes. Map: Natural Earth.
